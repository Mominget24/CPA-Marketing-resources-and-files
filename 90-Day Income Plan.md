# The 90-Day Plan — Income From Zero, Zero Spend

For HP. Bangladesh. 6+ hours a day. No current income. No money to invest. WordPress developer and AI builder who can ship a professional site for free on Cloudflare Pages.

Prepared 25 September 2026. Read `Course Review - Honest Verdict.md` first if you haven't.

## The reframe you need before anything else

You came to this wanting CPA marketing to be your only income source. I want to be honest with you about why that framing is the thing most likely to hurt you.

CPA is not a business. It is a **payment method** — one of several ways to monetise attention. Asking "how do I live on CPA" is like asking "how do I live on bank transfers." The business is the attention: an audience or a stream of visitors who trust you. Once you have that, CPA is one of the taps you can open, alongside affiliate commissions, display ads, and selling your own services. The reason the course had to teach fake identities is that it tried to skip the audience and go straight to the tap.

You have something almost nobody in that class had: **a skill that people pay for today.** You build websites. In Bangladesh that is worth real money this month, not in six months. That skill is your bridge, and it also happens to be the exact foundation the legitimate version of CPA needs — because to run CPA honestly you need a real site with real visitors, and you can build one for free.

So the plan has two tracks running at the same time, and the order matters enormously:

**Track A — Runway.** Freelance web development. Money in weeks, not months. This exists to take the desperation out of the situation, because desperation is what makes fraud look reasonable.

**Track B — Asset.** A content property in a niche where you have genuine expertise, monetised with affiliate and CPA offers. Slow to start, compounds forever, and cannot be banned because you own it.

Track A funds Track B. Track B eventually replaces Track A. Do not skip Track A.

## Week 1: the unglamorous foundation

### Start your Payoneer application on day one

This is the most important thing in the first week and it has nothing to do with marketing. **Payoneer KYC is the bottleneck for every single income source in this plan** — Fiverr withdrawals, Upwork withdrawals, CPAGrip payouts, affiliate payouts. It can take days or weeks to clear, and it will block you at the exact moment you finally have money to collect. Start it now so it is finished before you need it.

Class 11 of your course got this right, so reuse it. What you need ready: your NID, a bank account in **exactly** the same name as your NID, a 2 to 3 month bank statement, and an electricity bill as proof of address. One Payoneer account per NID — do not create a second one for any reason. You upload the NID by scanning a QR code with your phone.

If you do not have a bank account yet, open one this week. Nothing else in this plan works without it.

Do not use the instructor's PayPal. Do not use anyone's PayPal. Your money goes to your account under your name, always, with no exceptions and no 5% shortcuts.

### Build three demo sites — this is your entire portfolio

Pick three local business types that exist on every street in Dhaka and that mostly have no website or a terrible one. My suggestions: a **restaurant**, a **dental or diagnostic clinic**, and a **real estate / flat rental agency**. You could equally pick a coaching centre, a beauty parlour, a law chamber, or a small manufacturer.

Build one complete, genuinely good site for each, on Cloudflare Pages, with AI doing the heavy lifting. Use invented but *clearly fictional* business names — this is a portfolio piece, not an impersonation, so do not use a real business's name and branding without permission. Each should have a homepage, services, gallery, about, contact form, a WhatsApp click-to-chat button, and a Bengali/English toggle if you can manage it. Mobile-first, because your clients' customers are all on phones.

Your delivery cost is zero. Understand how unusual that is. Every competitor bidding against you has to price in hosting. You do not.

### Build a one-page portfolio site

On a `.pages.dev` subdomain. Your real name, your real photo, your real phone number and WhatsApp. The three demos linked with screenshots. A clear price list. A sentence saying you deliver in 48 hours.

Put your real identity on everything. This is not a moral point, it is a commercial one — local clients pay strangers on trust, and a real face with a real Bangladeshi phone number converts far better than anything anonymous.

### Get your channels open

Join ten to fifteen Facebook groups: Bangladeshi freelancer groups, Dhaka small-business owner groups, "buy and sell in [your area]" groups, and any local trade associations. Don't post yet. Read for a week and learn what people ask for and what they pay.

Create a Fiverr profile using your three demos as the gig images. Fiverr is free to join and does not need Payoneer until you withdraw. Create two or three narrow gigs — "I will build a professional WordPress restaurant website in 2 days" beats "I will do web development." Upwork is worth a profile too, but it is slower and more competitive, so treat Fiverr and local clients as the priority.

### Time budget for week 1

Roughly four hours a day on the demos and portfolio, one hour on Payoneer and the bank, one hour reading the groups.

## Weeks 2–4: get paid

### Your unfair advantage is building before you pitch

Everyone else sends a message saying "I can build you a website." You are going to send a message saying "**I already built you one — here it is.**"

Walk or scroll through your area and list twenty local businesses with no website or an embarrassing one. Pick the five best prospects. For each, spend two hours with AI building a real, specific site for *their actual business* — their real name, their real menu or services, photos from their Facebook page, their real address and phone. Then message the owner: *"I noticed you don't have a website, so I built one for you. Here's the link. If you like it, it's yours for [price] and I'll have it live on your own domain in two days. If not, no problem, keep the link."*

Two honest notes on this. Take the photos and business details from the owner's own public Facebook page, and say so — you are showing them their own material arranged well, and if they decline you should take the demo down rather than leave it up. Also mention up front that the domain and hosting are a separate small cost they'll pay directly to the registrar, so there is no surprise later and no cost to you.

The conversion rate on a finished product is not comparable to the conversion rate on a pitch. Expect roughly one in five to convert, sometimes better, because you have removed all of the imagination and all of the risk from their side.

### Pricing

For local Bangladeshi small businesses, something in the range of 8,000 to 20,000 taka for a small business site is realistic and fair for 48-hour delivery — check what your local groups are actually quoting and position yourself in the middle, not at the bottom. Underpricing attracts the worst clients and makes you look inexperienced.

Take half up front via bKash or Nagad. Local payments are instant, which is why local clients are your fastest cash and international platforms are your second priority.

Then offer a maintenance retainer — 1,500 to 3,000 taka a month for updates, content changes, and backups. **Retainers are the only thing that turns freelancing from a treadmill into an income.** Three retainer clients is your rent covered whether or not you close anything new that month.

### Realistic expectation for the first month

One or two closed clients if you are pushing hard, so somewhere around 10,000 to 30,000 taka. Some people close nothing in month one. That is not failure, it is the pipeline filling up — the outreach you do in week 2 typically closes in week 4 or 5.

### Keep 90 minutes a day for Track B

Do not let the freelance work eat everything, or in three months you will have money but still no asset, and you will be exactly as trapped as you are now, just better fed.

## Track B: the asset that eventually replaces the job

### Choose the niche where you are actually an expert

Not travel. Not weight loss. Not "make money online." **Web development, WordPress, and building things with AI.**

Here is the argument, and it is mostly arithmetic. The course had you chasing $2 email submits at roughly one conversion per thirty clicks. A single web hosting referral commonly pays somewhere around $60 or more. **One hosting sale is worth about thirty email submits.** And it converts better, not worse, because you are recommending a tool you genuinely use to an audience with a real problem, rather than dangling a fake iPhone.

On top of that you can write this content honestly and fast, because it is your actual job. You will never run out of material, because every client project generates three articles. And the same content that earns affiliate commissions also brings you freelance clients, which means Track B feeds Track A while Track A funds Track B. Nothing in the course had that property.

Your existing travel site keeps its place — leave it live, it's a portfolio piece and a useful SEO experiment, and Travelpayouts will approve almost anyone if you want to put honest hotel and flight links on it. Just don't make it the main bet. Travel affiliate needs enormous traffic before it pays anything, and you have no particular authority on the Ural region.

### Publish where the audience already is

You have no domain and no traffic, so borrow both. This is the part that respects your zero-spend constraint completely.

**YouTube in Bengali.** This is, in my honest assessment, your single highest-leverage free channel. Bengali-language technical content — "build a business website with AI in 30 minutes," "Cloudflare Pages vs shared hosting," "how I get web design clients" — is in real demand and far less competitive than the English equivalent. Screen recording with OBS is free. No domain required. Affiliate links go in the description with a disclosure. The same videos bring freelance clients directly, and they prove your skill better than any portfolio.

**Medium, under your real name.** Ignore what the course told you here. Medium has built-in traffic, allows affiliate links with disclosure, and a real name with a real bio builds an actual reputation. A fake American persona builds nothing and gets deleted.

**Your own site on a `.pages.dev` subdomain.** The canonical home for your long-form tutorials. Google indexes and ranks subdomains fine. A handful of affiliate programs dislike subdomains, but the ones that matter for you mostly don't care. When your first freelance payment lands, a domain becomes a ~$9 business expense paid out of income rather than out of nothing — that's the natural moment to revisit it, and not before.

**Pinterest, one real account.** Legitimate and worth doing: one account, your real brand, real content, disclosed links, no rename tricks, no multi-accounting. Class 09's pinning mechanics are usable — the parts to drop are the multiple accounts and changing your name after a week.

### What to actually monetise with

Start with affiliate programs you can join today with no interview and no minimum traffic. Web hosting and WordPress tooling is the obvious cluster — hosting providers, page builders, SEO plugins, and AI tools all run affiliate programs, many through networks like Impact where each brand approves you individually. Apply honestly, describe your real site and real traffic, and expect some rejections early. Reapply once you have content.

Layer CPA in once you have traffic, not before. You already have a CPAGrip account from Class 01, so keep it in good standing and use it properly: real offers, honest ads, no deleted disclaimers, and only traffic sources the offer permits. For a technical audience, the CPA offers that convert honestly are software free trials, VPN trials, hosting trials, and tool signups — things your reader actually wants. MaxBounty is worth applying to once you have a live site with traffic; they interview applicants by phone and accept international affiliates, so be straightforward about who you are and what you'll promote.

Adsterra is legitimate as display monetisation on your own real site. What Class 12 did with it — inventing fake Apple and Nike giveaways to farm impressions — was the fraud. The network itself is fine.

### Cadence

Two pieces of content a week, every week, for twelve weeks. Twenty-four pieces. That is the whole strategy and it is genuinely all of it. SEO and YouTube both pay out on accumulation, and almost everyone quits at piece number six.

## The daily shape of your six hours

Three hours on Track A — finding clients, building speculative demos, delivering paid work. This is the urgent track and it should feel urgent.

Two hours on Track B — writing, recording, publishing. Protect this time. It is the only part of your day that compounds.

One hour on learning and admin — one specific skill or one specific problem, plus Payoneer, invoices, and follow-ups.

When a client deadline collides with your content slot, the content slot wins the argument at least three days a week. Otherwise Track B quietly dies and you will not notice for two months.

## What the numbers realistically look like

I am giving you ranges, not promises, and the low end of each range is a real possible outcome.

Month one is mostly setup: zero to about 15,000 taka, from perhaps one freelance client. Month two the pipeline starts working: roughly 15,000 to 40,000 taka, still essentially all freelance, with your first content published and earning nothing yet. Month three you should have repeat clients and maybe a retainer: roughly 30,000 to 70,000 taka, plus possibly your first few dollars of affiliate or CPA income — and those first few dollars matter far more as proof than as money.

By month six, a plausible shape is freelance work at 40,000 to 90,000 taka a month with three or four retainers, and the content asset producing somewhere between $50 and $250. By month twelve, if you published consistently, the asset side can plausibly reach $300 to $1,000 a month, at which point it starts to look like the thing you originally wanted.

Notice that the asset does not overtake the freelance income inside 90 days. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something. What 90 days buys you is a working income and a real asset that is eight publishing months old instead of zero.

## The rules that keep you out of trouble

These are not optional and they are not about being nice. Every one of them protects your ability to keep earning.

Use your real identity everywhere — one account per platform, your real name, your real face. Never buy or sell accounts. Never use temp mail for anything you intend to keep.

Never impersonate a company. No fake job postings, no fake giveaways, no borrowed logos. If an offer supplies a disclaimer, it ships with your ad, untouched.

Disclose affiliate relationships plainly: "I earn a commission if you buy through this link." Most programs require it and it costs you nothing in conversions, because people who trust you buy anyway.

Never use a VPN or proxy to reach geo-restricted offers, and never complete your own offers. This is the one thing your instructor was right about — and he still got his own account permanently frozen for trying it.

Your payment details go on your accounts and nobody else's. You do not withdraw through another person, and you do not let anyone withdraw through you. Never share a password with a teacher, a mentor, an admin, or a "support team."

Read each offer's allowed traffic sources and honour them. When you are unsure whether something is permitted, email the network and ask. A network that answers your compliance question is a network that will still be paying you next year.

And don't buy another course. You already have the mechanical knowledge. What you are missing is twelve weeks of publishing, and no one can sell you that.
