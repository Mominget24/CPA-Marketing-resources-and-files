## What the course actually teaches

### The core method is a fabricated American identity

This runs through Classes 03 through 12. The instructions are explicit: get a name and a US address from `fakenamegenerator.com`, register with temp mail, generate an American-looking face with AI, invent a date of birth, and set your country to United States. Class 10 states the objective in the instructor's own words — *"we must try to convince people this is NOT a fake ID."*

Class 06 goes further and teaches photo laundering: download a real person's photos from someone's account, then AI-regenerate them so a reverse image search won't match. That is using a real human being's likeness without their knowledge.

Class 05 and 06 teach a trick worth naming plainly: open the account under **your real name and your real NID date of birth** so that if the platform demands identity verification you can pass it once — then change the name to an American one afterwards. You are being told to spend your real, single, non-replaceable national identity document to certify an account that will then be used deceptively. If that account is later actioned for fraud, it is your NID attached to it.

### It teaches you to impersonate real companies

This is the most legally exposed part of the course, and it is not a small aside.

Class 07 has the instructor generate an AI recruiting video with full PepsiCo branding — "complete warehouse, full Pepsi logo" — under a "Now Hiring" banner, with the brand name carried through onto the landing page. He cycles through Walmart and FedEx the same way. Class 03 invents job listings with fabricated hourly rates, and says the $40/hour figure is there "just to get clicks."

Class 12 builds fake Apple and Nike $1,000 giveaway banners. The instructor spells out the evasion: *"we won't say they'll get it, we'll say they* may *get it."* The user submits their email address and receives nothing at all. The money comes from ad impressions.

Posting fake job advertisements under a real employer's name is a recognised fraud pattern, prosecuted in multiple jurisdictions, and the people who answer them are job seekers. Set aside the account-ban risk for a moment: this causes real harm to real people who are looking for work.

### It teaches you to delete the legal disclaimer

Class 05 is the single worst instructional moment in the course. The CPA offer supplies its own required disclosure — *"Please review the official terms before submitting your information. This promotion is not affiliated..."* — and the instructor deletes it on camera: *"I won't keep it, I'll cut it, we don't need it."*

That sentence is the only thing standing between "an ad" and "impersonating an official brand promotion." Removing it is an advertising-disclosure violation and an immediate termination clause in every CPA network's terms, including CPAGrip's and Adsterra's.

### It teaches platform evasion as a routine

VPN to spoof your location and reach US-only offers. One separate browser per account so fingerprinting can't link them. Friend requests paced at five at a time, a minute apart, ten a day, so spam detection doesn't fire. Post without links for a month to build trust, then slip the link into the comments — because, in his words, "the chance of getting suspended drops a lot." Multiple Gmail accounts to farm past free AI-tool quotas. Cloned apps so one phone runs four identities.

Class 04 also endorses **buying aged accounts** from sellers. Every one of these is a terms-of-service breach on the platform in question.

### The instructor's own results show the method failing

He is not hiding this, which is the one honest thing about the course.

His three demo Bluesky accounts: *"all suspended."* His CPAGrip account, after he tried automated clicks: permanently frozen. His Pinterest numbers: 37 and 389 views, around 400 reach, zero followers. His conversion screenshots: 35 clicks to 1 lead, 21 to 1, 9 to 2, and a FedEx job ad that got 41 clicks and **zero** conversions.

Class 08 simply ends at 17 minutes because the power went out, and the remainder was never recorded. You paid for that class.

### He has financial interests pointed at you

He sells students US dollars at 125 taka. He sells ad credits at roughly $25 per classified post, while openly admitting *"maybe there's profit, maybe there's loss."* He collects your earnings screenshots. Class 01 asks you to send him the email **and password** of your Recyclearars and WebBuilder Pro accounts over WhatsApp.

And Class 11 contains the worst item in the entire course: he offers to have your payouts withdrawn **into his own PayPal account**, taking 5%. *"I'll give you my PayPal account. I'll give you the email."*

Never do this. Putting a third party's payment details on your affiliate account violates the terms of every network and is grounds for forfeiting your balance. More simply: you would be handing your only income to someone else's account and trusting him to pass it back, with no contract and no recourse. Do not share your passwords with him either. A teacher who needs your password is not teaching you.

## The parts worth keeping

### Class 01 — how to register properly

This is accurate and hard to find elsewhere in Bengali, so use it. Real name exactly as on your NID. Your **permanent** NID address. Country Bangladesh, city = district, state = division, ZIP = your thana postcode. Timezone Asia/Dhaka, GMT+6. Because Skype shut down in 2025, clear the "IM Service" field and put WhatsApp with your WhatsApp number. Never use temp mail. Click the verification email **within ten minutes** or that account can never be approved. Expect 24 to 48 hours for approval.

Note the irony worth absorbing: the instructor correctly insists on complete honesty when the *network* is checking you, and then teaches dishonesty everywhere the *public* is looking. The networks are not the only party that matters.

Also worth crediting: he reads the network terms aloud and affirms them — *"I will not complete the offer myself, I will not use proxy or VPN or bot software to complete leads"* — and says *"I will do completely legal, authentic work."* He means it about self-dealing. He just doesn't extend it to the end user.

### Class 11 — getting paid in Bangladesh

The most useful forty minutes of the course. PayPal is not available to you in Bangladesh, so Payoneer is the route. CPAGrip pays out at a $50 minimum; Payoneer to a local bank costs about $3 and lands in 1 to 3 days, paid in taka. The second lead network has a $100 minimum, requested on the 1st or 2nd of the month, paid in 3 to 5 days. Adsterra pays on the 1st and 16th with a two-week hold.

Payoneer KYC, which is where people get stuck: your name must match your NID and your bank account **exactly**, only one Payoneer account per NID, you'll need a 2 to 3 month bank statement, an electricity bill works as proof of address, and you upload your NID by scanning a QR code with your phone.

**Two corrections you need.** First, he tells Bangladeshi students to enter an **ITIN** on the W-8 tax form. An ITIN is a US taxpayer number that you do not have and cannot casually get. The W-8BEN is signed under penalty of perjury, so entering an invented or borrowed ITIN is a false certification — a much worse problem than the one it solves. Use your Bangladesh eTIN in the foreign TIN field, or leave the US TIN field blank, which is normal and accepted for a foreign individual. Bangladesh has no US tax treaty, so there is no treaty benefit to claim anyway.

Second, he advises delaying your eTIN registration so you won't owe the roughly 2,000 taka. That is not tax planning, it is just deferral, and it works against you: a Payoneer-to-bank paper trail is the thing that makes your income *provably legitimate* — useful for loans, visas, and any future business registration. Bangladesh has historically given favourable treatment to IT and freelance export earnings received through legal banking channels, but the specifics change with each finance act. I am not a tax adviser and you should confirm the current position with a local accountant rather than take either his advice or mine as final.

## Was the course a waste of money?

Not entirely, and not in the way that matters most.

You now know how CPA actually works mechanically: what an email submit is, what a payout looks like, how a network dashboard is laid out, how offers are selected, what a landing page does in the chain, and how money physically reaches a Bangladeshi bank account. That knowledge is real and it transfers completely to the legitimate version of this business. Most beginners are missing exactly that, and you are not.

What you did not get is a sustainable traffic strategy. Every traffic method in the course depends on accounts that are designed to be thrown away, feeding offers through ads that need to deceive someone to work. There is no asset at the end of it. If you had followed the course exactly for six months, you would own nothing.

The good news is the specific reason that gap is fixable in your case: **you can build real websites for free, and nobody else in that class can.** That single skill lets you run the legitimate version of this business, which needs an owned asset and real traffic. The course's students don't have that option, which is probably why the course teaches what it teaches.

That is what the 90-day plan is built on.
